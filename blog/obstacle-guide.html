Obstacle Guide: Barriers, Drones and Meteors Explained

Three kinds of hazards end your run. Each one moves differently, and each has a reliable counter.

Barriers

Barriers stand on the road and scroll toward you at road speed. They vary in height, from about the height of the truck to roughly twice that. They never move on their own, so they are the most predictable hazard.

Counter: Start your burst a little before the barrier reaches you, so the truck is already rising as it arrives. For short barriers a single quick tap is enough. For tall ones hold slightly longer, then release as the rear wheels clear the top so you drop straight back to the road.

Drones

Drones hover in the air and drift up and down in a smooth wave. A full cycle takes a little over two seconds, and the drone moves up and down by nearly the height of the truck each way.

Counter: Do not aim for the height where you first saw the drone. Watch it for half a second, note whether it is rising or falling, and pass through the space it is about to leave. When two drones appear close together, flying low and staying on the road is often safer than trying to weave between them.

Meteors

Meteors enter from the top of the screen and fall diagonally toward the road. Their bright tail points back along the path they came from, so the tail shows you where they are going next.

Counter: Meteors are fast but they fall out of play when they reach the ground. If a meteor is dropping toward your lane, the road under it becomes dangerous only for a moment. Staying low and waiting a beat often beats trying to fly over it.

Combinations

Hazards are spawned at random with a gap between them that shrinks as your score rises. Late in a run you will see a barrier followed quickly by a drone. In that case clear the barrier with a short burst, then immediately drop back low rather than staying high, because the drone is likely to be at head height.
